# Research behind the synthesis

Researched 2026-11-01. These sources informed original instructions; this skill does not install upstream packages or require their tools. Repository descriptions are workflow examples, not evidence that a learning method works. No verbatim upstream instruction blocks are incorporated.

## Existing skills reviewed

- [FavioVazquez/agentic-learning — SKILL.md](https://github.com/FavioVazquez/agentic-learning/blob/main/SKILL.md): useful patterns include one-question retrieval, feedback tied to the answer, mixed practice, and later review. This synthesis applies those patterns beyond software learning and keeps a short default interaction instead of exposing a broad action catalog.
- [SkillMedev/skills — study-system](https://github.com/SkillMedev/skills/blob/main/skills/study-system/SKILL.md): useful patterns include small testable concepts, time-bounded planning, spaced review, and recording errors. This synthesis adds the learner's seven-day constraint and adapts the schedule without claiming to predict exactly when forgetting will occur.
- [evanshlee/feynman-technique — SKILL.md](https://github.com/evanshlee/feynman-technique/blob/main/SKILL.md): useful patterns include identifying one gap at a time, asking for explanation in the learner's words, and providing support when stuck. This synthesis avoids mandatory file logging, browser companions, and rigid claims of permanent mastery.

The user's ten requested study activities supply the mode set. Visual sticky notes, story length, the two-demonstration checkpoint, and the example review schedule are design choices for this workflow, not findings established by these repositories or studies.

## Learning research

### Retrieval and distributed practice

[Dunlosky et al. (2013), Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266).

This review rated practice testing and distributed practice as broadly useful across the conditions it assessed. It gave more conditional support to self-explanation and interleaving. Consequently, the core workflow asks the learner to retrieve or apply an idea and returns to it later. It does not give passive rereading equal priority. These are findings about the reviewed learning techniques, not proof that any particular AI tutor or prompt improves speed by a fixed factor.

### Retrieval alongside visual maps

[Karpicke & Blunt (2011), Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/).

In the studied science-text tasks, retrieval practice improved meaningful learning, including inference performance, relative to the concept-mapping comparison. This supports adding recall and application checks to visual aids. It does not establish that concept maps never help, or that retrieval alone teaches every practical skill.

### Review timing depends on the retention goal

[Cepeda et al. (2008), Spacing effects in learning: A temporal ridgeline of optimal retention](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/).

In this fact-learning study, the spacing gap associated with best performance varied with the delay until the final test. This motivates adaptable intervals and explicit retention goals. Days 1, 2, 4, and 7 are a convenient first-week example, not a schedule experimentally validated for every topic or learner. A seven-day plan also needs later checks if the goal is retention over months.

## Claims to avoid

- A guaranteed “10x” gain or a fixed learning multiplier.
- Exact “20% gives 80%” claims across all subjects.
- Treating stories, sticky notes, or three mental models as equally supported substitutes for retrieval and practice.
- Inferring a fixed visual, auditory, or kinesthetic learner identity from a preference or a few chat answers.
- Certifying lasting mastery from same-session performance, confidence, or successful repetition after a hint.

When explaining the method, distinguish published findings from workflow heuristics and the learner's observed results. For improvement claims about an individual, compare meaningful baseline and later performance on comparable, unfamiliar tasks; do not manufacture measurements.
